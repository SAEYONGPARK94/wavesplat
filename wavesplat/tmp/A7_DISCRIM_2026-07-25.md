## Verdict — **A2 ≈ A1** (strictly per the pre-registered read-out)

The frozen read-out: *"if A2 ≈ A1, the mechanism is the fine-atom bank refinement and
'refine-select' is dead weight (method must be renamed/re-described); if A2 collapses to A3,
refine-select matters through a path dk/k does not capture."*

A2 (max |Δfield0| = 0.005 vs A1, rho identical to 3 decimals, 5/5 recover) is
**statistically indistinguishable from A1** and **nowhere near A3** (mean field0 1.00 vs 0.34).
⟹ **A2 ≈ A1.** The working ingredient is the **fine-atom splice**; **refine-select is dead
weight** on these episodes — it costs M = 8 Nelder-Mead single-component optimisations per
frame for a ≤0.005 field-corr change.

## Corrected mechanism narrative

> **MEASURED.** On Nyquist-adequate BF2 1comp data, splicing a dk = kp/40 fine-atom bank into
> the MMSE bank recovers field corr 0.995–1.000 and encounter rho 0.90–0.96 on all 5 seeds —
> **whether the bank is centred on the continuous-k refine-select winner (A1) or on the plain
> beamform argmax (A2)**; the two differ by ≤0.005 in field corr and are identical in rho.
> Removing the splice (A3) collapses the result exactly onto the hand path (2/5, mean field0
> 0.336). Therefore the recovery is produced by the fine-atom bank densification, not by the
> refine-select / holdout-selection stage.
>
> **ARGUED.** Why the refine step is immaterial here: on adequate mesh the beamform argmax
> already lands within ~5 % of true |k| (the sub-Nyquist "sidelobes" that motivated
> refine-select were rendering aliases, per the 2026-07-23 retry). The fine grid's coverage
> radius (±25 % magnitude, ±15° angle) around the argmax comfortably contains the true k, so
> densifying atoms around the argmax is sufficient; Nelder-Mead relocation of the grid centre
> from ~5 %-off to ~0 %-off is a shift smaller than the grid's coverage and changes nothing
> the bank could not already resolve. This does **not** claim refine-select is useless in
> general — only that on this regime (adequate mesh, argmax already near true), it is not the
> operative ingredient. If the argmax were displaced beyond the ±25 %/±15° grid (e.g. a true
> sidelobe survivor), refine-select could matter; no such case occurs in these 5 seeds.

## Implication for any A-7 write-up

The method as demonstrated is a **fine-atom bank refinement around the beamform argmax**, not a
"refine-then-select" super-resolver. Any revival must (a) rename/re-describe accordingly, and
(b) drop the M continuous optimisations unless a regime is exhibited where the argmax lands
outside the fine grid. Scope caveats from the parent doc still hold: one synthetic corner case,
zero demonstrated real-data value (detector never fires on YS17).
