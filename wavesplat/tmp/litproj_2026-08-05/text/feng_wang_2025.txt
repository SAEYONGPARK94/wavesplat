A B S T R A C T
Accurate spatial–temporal wave measurement is vital for ocean engineering applications. Although stereo
vision shows great potential in this field, performing dense reconstruction requires processing vast amounts
of pixel data, which reduces the efficiency of stereo image matching and subsequent point cloud processing.
Recently, the paradigm of fusing sparse 3D points with predictions has emerged as a promising solution that
balances accuracy and efficiency, yet requires an optimization framework capable of handling both state
estimation and robust outlier filtering. Therefore, this study proposes a Kalman filter (KF)-based method
for ocean wave field estimation, aiming to improve efficiency through recursion and to remove outliers
and interpolation errors in measurements. The method leverages linear gravity wave dispersion relations for
prediction, with sparse 3D points interpolated to a uniform grid as measurements. To address limitations
of high-dimensional data processing, the study implements the Ensemble Transform Kalman Filter (ETKF),
incorporating fuzzy logic to handle potential outliers. By maintaining an ensemble of states and employing
ensemble transformation techniques to avoid computationally expensive matrix inversions, ETKF significantly
improves recursive processing efficiency. Both CPU and GPU implementations were evaluated on published and
field-collected datasets, demonstrating superior performance in efficiency, accuracy, and robustness compared
to existing methods under the same paradigm.
1. Introduction
Accurate spatial–temporal wave measurement (Benetazzo et al.,
2016; Li et al., 2022) is essential for ocean engineering applications,
ranging from navigation to offshore structure safety, and wave en-
ergy harvesting. Traditional single-point measurement systems cannot
adequately capture critical features of three-dimensional sea surfaces,
including directionality, wave height, and surface slope. Furthermore,
research on spatial–temporal fields based on three-dimensional wave
measurements has yielded new insights into wave modeling (Leckler
et al., 2015; Barbariol et al., 2017). Consequently, developing efficient
and robust spatial–temporal wave measurement methods has become a
research priority.
Wave measurement techniques have evolved significantly over the
past decades. Traditional methods relied on wave gauges and surface
buoys, which can provide precise time series but fail to capture the
spatial structure of wave fields. Researchers have explored multi-point
measurement arrays to address this limitation. For instance, Swan
and Sheikh (2015) deployed 1400 probes to map the spatial–temporal

stereo vision has shown great potential in this field, finding widespread
application in both outdoor water surfaces (Benetazzo, 2006; Benetazzo
et al., 2012) and laboratory wave tanks (Bechle and Wu, 2011; Li

The Wave Acquisition Stereo System (WASS) represents a significant
advancement in stereo vision wave measurement, being an open-source
software developed by Bergamasco et al. (2017) that combines modern
computer vision techniques to provide an effective tool for dense recon-
struction of ocean wave. The system encompasses the entire pipeline of
generating 3D point clouds using Semi-Global Block Matching (SGBM)
as introduced by Hirschmuller (2007), extracting sea level and eleva-
tion data and rendering ocean wave height onto images. WASS has
been widely utilized for wave characterization in various environments
such as the sea surface (Guimarães et al., 2019; Vieira et al., 2020) and
laboratory wave tanks (Benetazzo et al., 2019; Pereira et al., 2021).
However, wave reconstruction remains computationally intensive,
as dense reconstruction requires processing vast amounts of pixel data,
reducing efficiency in both stereo image matching and subsequent
processing including smoothing interpolation and outlier filtering. To
break through this bottleneck, Bergamasco et al. (2021) proposed
WASSfast, which aims at real-time wave field reconstruction using
sparse 3D points. The method transforms the spectrum estimation into a
constraint minimization problem, seeking an optimal balance between
the predicted spectrum and the elevation from the sparse 3D points.
To avoid overfitting from strict constraints, the soft constraint variant
was also explored in WASSfast. WASSfast employs an iterative gradient
descent instead of the closed-form solutions due to the ill-conditioned
matrices caused by data sparsity. Additionally, Pistellato et al. (2021)
developed a CNN mode of WASSfast that integrates physics prediction
with network architecture, trained on fixed 256 × 256 grids for direct
sparse-to-dense elevation mapping.
WASSfast not only establishes a new paradigm for wave field esti-
mation, but also enables reinterpretations within different theoretical
frameworks. From Bayesian perspectives, fusing prediction and mea-
surement data represents maximum a posteriori (MAP) estimation,
where the prediction model provides the prior and wave measurements
supply likelihood data. This MAP problem is solvable recursively via
Kalman filter (KF), which can estimate state uncertainty using iterated
Fisher information matrices. Indeed, researchers have explored wave
field estimation using KF. For instance, Pascoal et al. (2017) and Kim
(2020) proposed Kalman filter-based approaches for sea state estima-
tion, but using multiple ships concurrently as buoys. They constructed
the state vectors from wave components (paired real and imaginary
spectrum parts) evolving independently, typically using tens of entry
frequencies.
In stereo vision-based wave reconstruction, although point clouds
provide better spatial representation of wave fields, the high dimen-
sionality (𝑂(104) in this study) of the state vector flattened from the
2D wave elevation grid makes direct application of the Kalman fil-
ter impractical. To address this, we propose a spatial–temporal wave
field estimation method based on the ensemble transform Kalman
filter (ETKF). As an extension of the ensemble Kalman filter (EnKF),
ETKF has been widely used in large-scale data assimilation, particu-
larly for weather and ocean forecasting (Blockley et al., 2014; Zhou
et al., 2019). By maintaining an ensemble of states and using trans-
form techniques to avoid large-scale matrix inversions, the proposed
ETKF-based method significantly enhances processing efficiency. The
proposed method adopts WASSfast’s prediction model, and interpolates
sparse 3D points to a uniform grid of wave elevations as observations.
The main contributions of this paper are: (1) A theoretical comparison
between the frameworks of WASSfast’s constrained optimization and
MAP-based recursive Kalman filtering, under the paradigm of fusing
spectrum prediction with sparse stereo 3D reconstruction; (2) An ETKF
implementation for spatial–temporal wave field estimation with optical
sparse measurement, integrating recursive processing and fuzzy logic to
achieve efficient computation and outlier elimination.
The rest of this paper is organized as follows: Section 2 reviews
the theoretical framework of the new paradigm in WASSfast, and
examines the fundamental connection between the constrained opti-
mization problem and the Kalman filter framework. Section 3 details
the proposed fuzzy logic-enhanced ETKF method for estimating spatial–
temporal wave fields. Section 4 evaluates the method using a public
stereo image dataset of ocean waves (Guimarães et al., 2020) as well
as the field-collected data in a vessel on position keeping. Finally,
conclusions are given in Section 5.
2. Comparative analysis
This study begins with a brief overview of WASSfast, a paradigm
combines the sparse 3D point cloud with wave spectrum prediction and
updating. We aim to investigate the theoretical basis of the proposed
method by analyzing the relationship between the cost functions of the
Kalman filter and the constrained optimization in WASSfast.

3. Methodology
This section describes a methodology for ocean wave reconstruction
using the Ensemble Transform Kalman Filter (ETKF) framework. The
proposed method employs sparse 3D reconstruction to provide mea-
surements for an enhanced ETKF implementation, which incorporates
fuzzy logic for robust outlier handling. The measurement system and
filtering mechanism are presented.
3.1. The measurement in Kalman filter
3.1.1. Sparse reconstruction
The accurate 3D sparse reconstruction of ocean wave surfaces pro-
vides measurements for the Kalman filter over the defined grid area.

Fig. 1. Comparison of sparse and dense stereo reconstruction pipelines for ocean wave surface, showing the processing steps and resulting elevation grids.
Our goal is to efficiently obtain sparse 3D points through precise feature
matching between stereo image pairs. This feature-based approach
allows us to focus computational resources on the most distinctive and
reliable points of the water surface, rather than attempting to match
every pixel. Fig. 1 shows the process of wave field reconstruction using
sparse and dense matching points, respectively.
Firstly, feature detection is performed using the Shi-Tomasi corner
detector (Shi and Tomasi, 1994) to identify salient corner features
from image pairs. These features are typically located at wave crests,
troughs, and regions with significant intensity variations on the water
surface. In practice we found that this corner-based approach achieves
a good balance between computational efficiency and feature stability
compared to more complex detectors.
The second step involves feature matching. The Lucas–Kanade op-
tical flow (Bouguet, 2001) is utilized to track the detected features,
which has demonstrated robust performance in handling water surface
textures. To ensure matching reliability, feature matches with large
reprojection errors are eliminated. After filtering, thousands of reliable
feature correspondences are typically obtained.
The final two steps are 3D reconstruction and mapping to a 2D
grid. Using the camera parameters calibrated through the method
by Zhang (1999), we triangulate the matched features to obtain 3D
coordinates. During this process, we further apply geometric constraints
to filter out physically implausible points. For instance, considering
the measurement range and camera mounting height, the reconstructed
points should fall within reasonable elevation bounds.
3.1.2. Interpolated measurements
With sparse observation points available, two measurement strate-
gies are considered within the KF framework: direct assimilation of
sparse values and assimilation of linearly interpolated values. Both
options are examined in the experiments section. For the interpo-
lated measurements, we adopt a linear interpolation method based
on Delaunay triangulation, which achieves a good balance between
computational efficiency and interpolation accuracy. Specifically, for a
target point located within a triangle formed by vertices 𝐴(𝑥1, 𝑦1, 𝑧1),

Of course, it must be said that as a Monte Carlo method, the
success of the ETKF greatly depends on ensemble size and statistical
representativeness. Lorenc (2003) demonstrated that when ensembles
are generated by random sampling via the Probability Density Function
(PDF), the discrepancy of forecast error covariance scales inversely
with ensemble size 𝑀. Furthermore, Wang and Bishop (2003) pointed
out that when the number of ensemble perturbations is significantly
smaller than the projected directions of forecast error variance, Eq. (31)
underestimates the total analytical error variance due to insufficient
contributions from crucial parts of the error space. In practical applica-
tions, computational constraints often prevent the ensemble size from
matching the state dimension (e.g., in this study the state dimension
is 16384 × 1 or 65536 × 1, while only tens to hundreds of ensemble
members are used). To address this issue, we employ a localization
strategy for ETKF, focusing on sub-vectors of states and observations,
which can also enable GPU-accelerated parallel processing. Fig. 2 illus-
trates the localization process. Specifically, the state vector is localized
into 𝐿sub-states that can be updated in parallel and isolation, where
each sub-state vector has a dimension of (𝑁∕𝐿, 1). The ensemble size
𝑀is typically set to be slightly larger than 𝑁∕𝐿to ensure adequate
sampling. While localized sub-states are used in the analysis step, the
forecast step requires a complete state vector corresponding to the ele-
vation grid for FFT operations. The localization shifts the computational
bottleneck from matrix inversion to SVD computation. Therefore, the
choice of 𝐿is a trade-off between parallel SVD efficiency and spatial
correlation preservation.

4. Experiments
We compared the proposed ETKF-based method with the results
obtained by WASS and WASSfast for different stereo sequences. The
data for the experiment include: the public Acqua Alta stereo data
set (AA 03), collected on the Acqua Alta oceanic research platform
with a 2.545 m camera baseline and 12.5 m height above sea level,
covering a measurement range of −25 m to 25 m in the x-direction
and 30 m to 80 m in the y-direction. The detailed setup parame-
ters refer to Guimarães et al. (2020). Additionally, we used a field-
collected dataset, gathered in March 2024 at a certain sea area with
a 0.12 m camera baseline and approximately 2 m height above sea
level, covering −3 m to 3 m in the x-direction and 5 m to 12 m in
the y-direction.
4.1. On the public dataset
We first processed all sequences using WASS with the recommended
parameters from the publish dataset to perform dense reconstruction as
ground truth. The accuracy of WASS dense reconstruction has been ex-
tensively validated in previous studies, showing centimeter-level errors
when compared with ultrasonic gauge measurements over long-term
monitoring periods (Benetazzo, 2006; Benetazzo et al., 2012, 2015).
The validation also demonstrated high spectral coherence in frequency
domain analyses. Then, we estimated the average sea surface by com-
puting the mean of corresponding surfaces for each frame and defined
a surface elevation grid, in which the interpolation was performed. The
PU mode of WASSfast based on the discussed constrained optimization
was performed on a 128 × 128 grid. For sparse field interpolation,
WASSfast also provides a CNN mode that performs direct fitting on a

the covariance matrix). Moreover, employing fuzzy logic helps prevent

on wave reconstruction, it is necessary to acquire wave images from

5. Conclusions
This study proposes an ETKF-based method for fast spatial–temporal
wave field reconstruction that combines spectrum predictions with
sparse 3D points, using fuzzy logic to filter out measurement outliers.
The proposed method inherently achieves global optimization through
MAP estimation, distinguishing it from traditional constrained opti-
mization methods with fixed weight matrices. Experiments on both
public datasets and field-collected data show superior performance in
multiple aspects: single-point elevation timeseries, 2D elevation grids,
and 3D spatial–temporal spectrums. The recursive uncertainty propa-
gation chain in ETKF significantly enhances computational efficiency
compared to the constrained optimization framework of WASSfast
(PU), and the parallel localization of state vector prevents the collapse
of covariance matrix in update.
Another important distinction lies in how the two frameworks han-
dle sparse data. While WASSfast directly uses sparse points as support
nodes for the fitting surface, the proposed method regards the entire
interpolated grid as the measurements, accounting for both the inter-
polation artifacts and measurement noise of the original sparse points
into the observation model within a filter. Through theoretical analysis
of innovation propagation and error evaluation, we demonstrate that
using interpolated measurements enhances state estimation not only
at directly observed locations but across the entire state space while
maintaining computational efficiency. The filtering mechanism effec-
tively handles the uncertainty of the interpolation, while leveraging
the expanded measurement space to better capture spatial correlations.
Furthermore, the proposed method maintains robust reconstruction
accuracy and comparable efficiency under extremely sparse conditions,
though at the higher dense ratio its computational efficiency is lower
than CNN mode of WASSfast. Future work could focus on exploring
appropriate entry frequencies as state vectors to reduce state dimen-
sionality, while preserving prior correlations through spectral–spatial
transformation in the mode of direct sparse assimilation.
